# Investor protections, limits and security: draft clauses for the lawyer

Status: DRAFT for legal review. Not legal advice. Square brackets [ ] are numbers the Company decides.
They match the settings in Admin > Deal Sheets > "Limits and protections shown to investors":
minimum shares [10], maximum per investor [100], lock-in [12] months, security covers [30] % for [24] months.
The deal sheet and the pitch show these numbers; if a clause here is changed, change the setting too.

## Why these clauses exist
Investors were asked: what happens if the business fails completely, and are there limits on who can hold and sell shares.
Honest position: a Profit Share is a right to profit. Nothing can promise profit or a return of money from a failed business.
What the Company CAN promise is how the money is handled, what comes back first, and what is pledged against dishonesty.

## 1. Use of funds and staged release
1.1 Money raised is spent only on the budget lines in Schedule [x] (reserve, marketing, promotion, bonuses, referral, materials).
1.2 Funds are released in steps. The steps and the result needed for each (for example drivers signed up, trips a day) are in Schedule [y].
1.3 Money not yet released stays in the Investor Account (clause 2). Spending outside the budget lines needs the written consent of holders of more than [50] % of the Profit Shares.

## 2. Separate accounts
2.1 Investor money is paid into a bank account used only for the raise (the Investor Account), in the name of [entity].
2.2 The reserve (about 26 % of Stage 1) is held in a separate account. Every withdrawal needs [two] signatures: [founder] and [second signatory].
2.3 The Company will not mix investor money with the money of other Etel companies.

## 3. Priority if the business stops
3.1 If the business closes or is wound up, the cash left after debts, staff, taxes and costs of winding up is paid to Profit Share holders first, in proportion to their shares, until each holder has received back the capital not yet returned to them (after counting payouts already received).
3.2 After that, anything left belongs to the Company.
3.3 Lawyer to confirm: how this ranks against creditors under Ethiopian law, and whether the Investor Account can be ring-fenced.

## 4. Reporting and audit
4.1 A statement to each holder every [month/quarter] showing net profit, the pool, the holder's amount and the launch budget against spending.
4.2 An independent accountant reviews the books once a year and the summary is given to every holder.
4.3 Each holder has a personal portal login and read-only platform data as already provided.

## 5. Founder pay and related-party payments
5.1 The founder's pay is capped at [ETB x per month] until the business reaches break-even.
5.2 Any payment to the founder, family or a company they control, beyond that pay, is disclosed in the next statement and needs the consent in clause 1.3 above [ETB y].

## 6. Limits on holders
6.1 Minimum purchase: [10] Profit Shares. The last remaining shares of a stage may be bought below the minimum.
6.2 Maximum for one investor (all sheets together, matched by ID number): [100] Profit Shares.
6.3 The Company may refuse a sheet only for a stated reason (identity not verified, source of funds not lawful, limit exceeded).

## 7. Lock-in and transfer
7.1 No sale or transfer for [12] months after enrolment.
7.2 After that, a holder may sell to a person the Company approves. Approval may not be refused without a good reason, and the Company answers within [30] days.
7.3 The Company has the first right to buy at the price set by the formula in Schedule [z] (for example the holder's unrecovered capital plus [x] % of payouts due, to be agreed).
7.4 Inheritance is always allowed. The Company registers the heir after seeing the court or family-council documents.
7.5 Once a year the Company opens a [14]-day window for holders to offer shares to the Company or to approved holders.
Good side: keeps the group known and stable and stops strangers or competitors from joining. Bad side: it makes the share harder to sell, so keep clause 7.2's "good reason" and 30-day answer.

## 8. Security (collateral) given by the founder
Founder's wish: in a country where trust is built on visible security, register personal assets so investors are protected.
Recommended structure, three layers:
8.1 **Security against misuse, always.** Named assets [car: plate/chassis, house: title deed/plot no., other] are registered as security for repayment of any investor money that is misused, hidden or paid out in breach of this Agreement, without limit of time while the Agreement runs.
8.2 **Limited backstop for business failure.** The same assets also secure [30] % of each holder's unrecovered capital for the first [24] months, reducing in steps as the business reaches the trip targets in Schedule [y]. After the period the backstop ends and only 8.1 remains.
8.3 **Company assets first.** Equipment and receivables of the business are pledged before personal assets are called on.
8.4 Mechanics for the lawyer: registration at the proper offices for each asset type; valuation by an independent valuer; spouse or co-owner consent for the house; no existing mortgage on the pledged assets (or ranking agreed); the car kept insured with the holders named; the founder may not sell or re-pledge the assets while the security stands; release in writing when the security ends; how and how fast it can be enforced.
8.5 Risks to explain to the founder: pledged assets can be lost; enforcement is slow and forced sales often fetch much less than value; full security for business failure would make the deal look like a loan (tax and company-law effect). The percentage in 8.2 is therefore limited on purpose.
8.6 The security is only shown to investors when the Company has really registered it and switched "Registered security is in place" on in Admin.

## 9. Events of default
Misuse of funds, false statements, failure to deliver statements, breach of clause 5, or insolvency. Consequences: holders may require the clause 8 security to be enforced and the Investor Account to be frozen by holders of more than [50] % of the shares.

## 10. Disputes and law
Governing law: Ethiopia. First a meeting within [30] days, then arbitration in [Hawassa / Addis Ababa] under [rules], language [English and Amharic; Amharic text controls?]. Lawyer to confirm.

## 11. Risk statement (also on the deal sheet)
These protections reduce risk. They do not guarantee profit or the return of the investor's money. A Profit Share is not a share in the Company and carries no vote.

## Questions investors ask (short answers)
- *Is my money guaranteed?* No. Profit is never guaranteed. If the business closes, the cash left after debts, staff and taxes comes to you first, in proportion, until your unrecovered capital is repaid; the reserve is in a separate two-signature account; spending is released in stages; and registered security protects against misuse [and, for a limited time, a share of unrecovered capital].
- *Can one person own everything?* No: maximum [100] shares each, minimum [10].
- *Can I sell?* After [12] months, to a person the Company approves (not refused without a good reason); the Company has the first right to buy at the formula price; heirs are always accepted.
- *What if the founder takes the money?* That is misuse: clause 8.1 security applies and the Investor Account can be frozen.
